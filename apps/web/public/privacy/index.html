Gravoth Privacy Policy

Effective 17 November 2026; updated 22 November 2026 for version 0.13, which removes Google Play Games, and for Gravoth Online, the optional accounts, leaderboards and friends at gravoth.petarzarkov.com. Applies to the Android game Gravoth (package com.petarzarkov.gravoth) and to Gravoth Online, both made by their independent developer, petarzarkov.

In short

  • Gravoth is a single-player game that plays fully offline. The game itself has no accounts.
  • Gravoth Online, at gravoth.petarzarkov.com, is optional: an account (or a guest) for the online leaderboards, whose records show each hero's full build, and a friends list. It keeps your account, your profile and the heroes and records you post on the developer's own server. See "Gravoth Online".
  • From version 0.13 the game no longer uses Google Play Games: it does not sign you in, keep your save in your Google account, record achievements on your Play Games profile or send scores to Play Games leaderboards. Once, when version 0.13 first starts on a phone, it reads the copy of your save that earlier versions kept in your Google account (if there is one), so you can choose to bring those heroes over; it never writes to it. Versions up to 0.12 used Google Play Games for sign-in, that cloud save, achievements and leaderboards. See "Google Play Games (versions up to 0.12, and the one last read)".
  • Nobody's personal data is sold. The game itself does not send your game data to the developer; Gravoth Online keeps only what you give it or post to it.
  • There are no ads and no tracking in the game, and no analytics or crash-reporting services are turned on.
  • The Google Play version offers optional supporter purchases, each a thank-you that comes with a cosmetic item with no effect on play. Google Play handles the payment; the game never sees your payment details. See "Supporter purchases".
  • In the game, apart from the one read of an earlier cloud save (see above) and what Google Play handles when you buy a supporter tier (see below), the only data that leaves your phone is the Unity engine's anonymous hardware statistics (the device's model and specs), which go to Unity, not to the developer. See "The game engine".

What stays on your phone

Your characters, items, progress and settings are saved only in the game's private storage on your device. The developer cannot see or access them. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data deletes them.

If you have Android's backup turned on, Android may include the game's saves in your device backup, which Google stores in your Google account under your own backup settings. The developer has no access to these backups. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings.

Google Play Games (versions up to 0.12, and the one last read)

Version 0.13 removes Google Play Games. Your achievements (MENU > FEATS) and your best scores (the BOARDS list, your own bests on this phone) are kept in the game on your phone, and your save stays on your phone (SETTINGS > SAVE TRANSFER moves it to another phone).

  • The one last read (version 0.13): the first time version 0.13 starts on a phone (and on up to two more launches if Google Play Games cannot be reached), it uses the Play Games sign-in that Google makes when the game starts to read the saved game that earlier versions kept in your Google account, if there is one. If it differs from the save on your phone, the game shows both and asks which one to keep; it never replaces either without your choice, and it never writes to the saved game. After that it does not contact Google Play Games again. During that sign-in Google Play Games collects its own data, as described below.

Versions up to 0.12 used Google Play Games as follows. On Android, if you had a Google Play Games profile, Google Play Games signed you in when the game started. Signing in was optional: if you declined or were offline, the game played the same, without cloud saves.

  • What the game received from Google: your Play Games player ID and gamer name and, in the BOARDS list, each leaderboard's top scores with the players' gamer names and the hero summaries sent with them. It did not receive your email address, your friends list or your contacts, and none of this was sent to the developer; the developer runs no server.
  • What was stored in your Google account: one saved game for Gravoth, holding the same data as the save on your phone (your characters, items, stash, codex and progress, the time you have played and when the save was made), plus a short description such as "Grom, level 24 (3 characters)", sent over an encrypted connection while you played. Google keeps it in the hidden application data of your Google account (Google Drive's app data, which does not use your Drive storage space or show in your files), up to 3 MB, until you delete it (see below).
  • Achievements: while you were signed in, the game told Google Play Games which achievements you had unlocked and your progress towards the counted ones, so they showed on your Play Games profile.
  • Leaderboards: while you were signed in, the game sent your best endgame scores (the deepest Bone Pit floor, the highest chart tier, the fastest tier-16 map clear, the most Marks and their hardcore twins) to Gravoth's Google Play Games leaderboards, each with a short summary of the hero who set it (a "score tag" of at most 64 characters: the hero's name, level, rites, main skill and its supports, movement skill, damage per second and life). Google showed them with your Play Games gamer name and avatar, under your Play Games profile's visibility settings.
  • What Google collects: as with any game that uses Play Games, Google Play Games collects your gamer profile (gamer name and avatar) and its own analytics and diagnostics data to keep its service working and improve it, under Google's Privacy Policy. The developer does not receive this data.
  • How to delete it: Google lets you delete your Play Games data (your saved game, achievements and leaderboard scores), for one game or your whole Play Games profile, from your Play Games profile or your Google Account. You can also remove the game's hidden saved data from Google Drive on the web (Settings, Manage apps, then Gravoth if it is listed, Options, Delete hidden app data). Deleting the cloud copy does not delete the save on your phone; uninstalling the game or clearing its data does.

Gravoth Online

Gravoth Online is optional: accounts, online leaderboards whose records show each hero's full build, and a friends list. It runs on the developer's own server (gravoth-api.petarzarkov.com, in the European Union) and is used by the website gravoth.petarzarkov.com. A later version of the game will use it too, and this policy will be updated before that version is released. Nothing in the game needs it. You can look at the leaderboards without an account; what follows applies once you sign in or play as a guest.

  • Your account: an email address, a name, and either a password, which the server keeps only as a one-way hash (bcrypt), or a sign-in with Google or Discord. A guest account has neither, only a random id and a placeholder address. Signing up from a guest keeps its heroes and display name and removes the guest.
  • Google and Discord sign-in: when you choose one, it asks your permission and then gives the server your account id at that service, your email address and whether the service has confirmed it, your name and the web address of your profile picture (Google's openid, email and profile permissions; Discord's identify and email). The server also keeps the sign-in tokens the service returns. It uses none of this to read anything else from your Google or Discord account or to act for you, and it never sees your password there. Google and Discord handle the sign-in under their own policies: Google's Privacy Policy and Discord's Privacy Policy.
  • Signing in: each sign-in keeps a session with the device's IP address, its browser or app identity (the user agent) and when it ends (after 90 days without use). On the website the session is one cookie (HttpOnly and Secure), and a second one guards a Google or Discord sign-in for the 5 minutes it takes. The site sets no other cookies: no tracking and no advertising.
  • Your profile: a display name other players see (3 to 20 letters, digits, _ or -, checked against a word filter), and your settings: whether your name shows on the leaderboards, whether others may send you friend requests, whether friends may see that you are online, and whether friend requests are mailed to you.
  • Heroes and records: when you post a record (such as the deepest Bone Pit floor or a chart clear), the server keeps the hero's name, class, level and whether it is hardcore; a snapshot of its whole build at that time (skills and supports, gear with every modifier, talents, atlas, auras, rites and cosmetics, and computed figures such as damage per second and life); the score; and when it was set. A record can come with the hero's save, so that the rules checker on the same server (the game's own rules) can confirm it. The save is read in memory and not kept; only the checker's figures and its build card are. A record that fails the check is kept for review and never shown.
  • Friends and blocks: the friend requests you send and receive (by display name), your friends and the players you block. Only you see these lists. Guests have no friends list.
  • What other players see: on the leaderboards, your display name, each hero's name, class and full build, the score and its date. If you hide your name (Account, privacy settings), the leaderboards show "A hidden player" in its place, but the hero's name and build still show. Friends see your display name.
  • Mail: the server sends mail through Resend, an email delivery service: to confirm your address, to reset a password, to confirm a deleted account and, if you allow it and your address is confirmed, for a friend request. Resend receives the address and the mail itself (your name and, for a friend request, both display names).
  • Security records: the server counts requests per IP address to slow down abuse and forgets each count within an hour. Its logs record each request (the address asked for, the time, the browser or app identity and the result), never passwords, session tokens or cookies. The logs have a fixed size and are overwritten as they fill.
  • Where it is kept: in the server's database. Cloudflare carries all traffic to the server and hosts the website (it sees visitors' IP addresses, as any network in front of a website does), and stores a nightly copy of the database in Cloudflare R2 for 30 days, so the server can be restored after a failure. Cloudflare handles this under Cloudflare's Privacy Policy. No other company receives Gravoth Online data, and there are no ads and no analytics.
  • Deleting your account: on the website, choose Account, then Delete my account (see Delete your account), or write from the account's address to gravoth.arpg@gmail.com (done within 30 days). It removes at once the account and its sign-ins and sessions, your profile, heroes, builds and records, and your friends, requests and blocks. The nightly copies that still hold them are deleted as they turn 30 days old, and log lines naming the account's id are overwritten as the logs fill. The heroes on your phone are not affected.
  • Your rights: you can see and change your display name and settings on the Account page, and delete the account at any time. For a copy of your data, a correction, or any other request about it (including your rights under the GDPR), write to gravoth.arpg@gmail.com. The server keeps this data to provide the service you signed up for, and the security records to protect it (a legitimate interest).

What the game does not do

  • It does not ask for your name, email address, contacts, location, photos, camera or microphone.
  • It contains no advertising and does not use the advertising ID.
  • Apart from the engine's hardware statistics, it contains no analytics, crash-reporting or tracking services. The game itself has no social features: from version 0.13 the BOARDS list shows your own bests on your phone only, and there is no chat or contact between players (the online leaderboards and friends are Gravoth Online's, above).
  • It sells nothing that changes the game. The in-game gambler sells random items for gold earned in play only; gold and items can never be bought with real money.

The game engine

Gravoth is made with the Unity engine. Unity's analytics and crash-reporting services are turned off. The engine itself sends Unity anonymous hardware statistics when the game runs: facts about the device such as its model, operating system, processor, graphics chip, memory and screen. This cannot be turned off in the edition of Unity the game is made with. It contains nothing you type or do in the game, and the developer does not receive it. Unity handles it under Unity's Privacy Policy. Apart from that and Google Play (the in-app update check, Google Play Games and the billing service for the optional supporter purchases, all handled by Google's own libraries), the game makes no network connections; it declares Android's standard internet permission, which the engine includes.

Google Play

When you download Gravoth from Google Play, Google handles the download, updates (the game asks Google Play whether an update is waiting) and any feedback you send through Play, under Google's Privacy Policy. The developer receives only the aggregate statistics and crash reports that Google Play Console provides to all developers, which do not identify you. If you join a test, the developer sees the Google account email you were invited with, only to manage the test list.

Supporter purchases

The Google Play version of Gravoth offers four optional supporter purchases on its SUPPORT GRAVOTH screen, opened from the in-game menu (Coffee, Lamp oil, Candle crate and Patron). Each gives one cosmetic item for your hero (Ember Edge, Lantern Halo, Candlewalk or Ashwings): a look only, with no stats, no gold, no boosts and no advantage. You can buy any of them as often as you like, and the game plays the same if you never do.

  • Google Play handles every purchase: the payment, your payment details and the receipt, under Google's Privacy Policy and Google Payments' terms. The game never sees your payment details.
  • To finish a purchase, the game receives from Google Play which supporter tier was bought, whether it is paid or still pending, and a purchase token, and hands the token back to Google Play to complete the purchase so it can be bought again. The game does not send any of this to the developer.
  • The game keeps a count of how many times you have supported it, on your phone, to say thank you; it is not part of your save and is deleted with the game's data. The cosmetic items you receive are kept in your save like any other item, together with a short one-way code per purchase so the same purchase never gives a second copy; they travel with your Google Play Games cloud save if you use it. No price, order number, purchase token or payment detail is kept in the save.
  • As for any Google Play purchase, Google gives the developer the order records Play Console shows (such as the order number, date, product and price), which the developer uses only for accounting and refunds.

The game only contacts Google Play's billing service when you open the SUPPORT GRAVOTH screen, or at launch to finish a purchase you started earlier that Google Play has not completed yet (for example a payment that was still pending). Copies of the game installed from outside Google Play offer no purchases.

Children

Gravoth is a dark-fantasy game with combat violence and is not directed at children. The game collects no personal data from anyone, including children; Google Play Games is subject to Google's own rules for children's accounts. Gravoth Online accounts are for players aged 16 and over; if the developer learns that an account belongs to a younger child, it is deleted.

Changes

If the game or Gravoth Online starts collecting or sending any other data (for example when a version of the game adds online features), this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the update notes.

Contact

Questions about this policy, or a request about your data: gravoth.arpg@gmail.com (also shown on Gravoth's Google Play page).
